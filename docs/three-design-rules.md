# ILoveAIImg's three design rules, and what they mean in daily use

Source: [About ILoveAIImg](https://iloveaiimg.com/about) · Workspace: [ILoveAIImg AI image tools](https://iloveaiimg.com/)

The About page describes ILoveAIImg as a workspace for "the practical work between an idea and a delivered file". It names three things the team cares about. Here is how each one shows up when you actually use it.

## 1. Less repetition

> A useful result should become the starting point for the next step, not another file you have to download and upload again.

In practice: generate in [Text to Image](https://iloveaiimg.com/text-to-image-free), then continue the same result in [Image to Image](https://iloveaiimg.com/image-to-image) or the [AI Image Editor](https://iloveaiimg.com/ai-image-editor) without re-uploading.

## 2. Results you can depend on

The workspace keeps source images, references and settings with the task, so you can compare versions and mark the final one before download.

In practice: keep one task per deliverable, mark the version you send, and download it in the format the destination needs.

## 3. A tool that stays understandable

Advanced models are reached through a direct task rather than a wall of controls. The [model list](https://iloveaiimg.com/ai-image-models) covers FLUX 3 Image Klein, GPT Image 2 and 2.5, Nano Banana (Lite, 2, Pro) and Seedream 5.0 Lite and Pro, each with a visible credit quote.

## What the team says it will not do

Choose feature clutter over useful workflows, make inflated promises, or measure success by time spent in the interface. The stated measure is whether you finish good work and move on with your day.
